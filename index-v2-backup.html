What your site is costing you — Phoenix Sun —

I put together a free audit of the tweaks I'd make, plus a quick look at how your business shows up when someone searches for what you do. I went through your agent page on huntingtonandellis.com and your own site, lasvegasluxuryrealtor.com, the way a buyer or seller who just searched for a Las Vegas agent would: land on the page, work out who you are and what you've done, then see how to reach you. The leaks are in the details: your own site still introduces you at your previous brokerage, the credentials in your email signature appear on neither site, “Read More” under your reviews opens the company-wide testimonials page, your agent page shows prices for 5 of 13 sold homes and no dates, its LinkedIn icon opens a sign-in screen, and your own site loads slowly on a simulated phone. Checked October 18, 2026

What I found

01

Your own site introduces you at your previous brokerage.

The first paragraph under the headline on lasvegasluxuryrealtor.com reads “Phoenix Sun is the team lead of the Sun Group at NV Broker & Associates and a top-producing agent honored as Las Vegas Inc.'s 2021 Industry Leading Agent.” The same page's contact block gives phoenix@huntingtonandellis.com and the 9525 W Russell Rd address, your About page gives your license number, and the description of your agent page on huntingtonandellis.com reads “REALTOR® | The Craig Tann Group”. So a visitor who reads the top of your own site meets a different brokerage than the one in your email signature. I only read what the pages say, and I can't tell whether the paragraph is meant as history. I read both on October 18, 2026.

02

The credentials in your email signature are on neither site.

Your email signature reads “S.0187108 | VA Rep, AHWD, ABR, SRS”. Your license number is on both sites, but the letters VA, AHWD, ABR and SRS don't appear on your agent page, your home page or your About page, and neither does the word “veteran” or “military”. When I searched “Las Vegas real estate agent VA loan specialist”, the answer named Christopher DeWitt and Dan Merrill, who both say it in their page text. I can't tell what each designation covers or whether you want it shown; I'm only reporting that your own pages are silent on it.

03

“Read More” under your reviews doesn't open your reviews.

Your agent page shows five client reviews, each cut off with “...” and each followed by “READ MORE”. All five of those links go to huntingtonandellis.com/testimonials, the company-wide page. Its first page, which I read, shows reviews of other agents, and its page selector runs to 220 pages. I didn't read all 220, so I can't say whether yours appear on it. A visitor who taps “Read More” on one of your reviews lands in a long list that starts with someone else's. This is a link on your brokerage's site, so it's a request for your broker's web team.

04

Your agent page shows prices for 5 of your 13 sold homes and no dates.

Under “Sold Properties” your agent page lists 13 homes. Five show a price, from $950,000 for 2178 Monte Bianco Pl down to $382,500 for 8649 Horizon Wind Avenue 102. The other eight show only beds, baths and square feet. None shows a sale date. Your own site's “Past Transactions” section shows both: “SOLD ON JUNE 30, 2026”, 611 Green Sage Way, $1,300,000, and “SOLD ON MARCH 26, 2026”, 2178 Monte Bianco Pl, $950,000. 611 Green Sage Way doesn't appear in the agent page's list at all. I don't know whether leaving prices off is deliberate.

05

Your agent page sends visitors to the company's pages, and its LinkedIn icon opens a sign-in screen.

The social icons on your agent page are Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn, and all four are the company's. Your own Zillow profile (5.0 from 11 reviews when a web search showed it on October 18, 2026) and the Instagram link in your email signature aren't linked from it, and your agent page and your own site don't link to each other. The LinkedIn icon, in the footer of every page on huntingtonandellis.com, goes to linkedin.com/company/10797341/admin/dashboard/, the admin page for the company's LinkedIn page. When I opened that address without being signed in, LinkedIn sent me to its sign-in page. The footer is the brokerage's, so that one is a request for your broker's web team.

06

Your own site loads slowly on a simulated phone.

In one Lighthouse run on a simulated slow phone, lasvegasluxuryrealtor.com scored 48 for performance, with the main content showing at 5.0 seconds and about 1.4 seconds of the page unable to respond while it loaded. Your agent page scored 66, with about 2.1 seconds of that, and the page weighs 6,253 KiB. Both use the same website platform. These are lab numbers from a desktop simulation, not measurements from real visitors, and I ran each page once.

What's working

You have the proof a seller looks for, and it's real: five client reviews that say what you did, such as one about a home that sold within 3 days of going live on the MLS and one about a condo that had sat three months before you took it over. Your phone number, email and license number are on the pages and match your signature, your number and email are tap-to-call and tap-to-email links on your agent page, and the forms carry a plain opt-in statement. Your own site reads like you: a bio about your design background and travels, a Past Transactions section with dates and prices, and page titles and descriptions that say Las Vegas. Both sites have a sitemap, a robots.txt, and one canonical address per page. Your agent page is listed in the company's sitemap and in its llms.txt file.

Unmeasured

Rankings, visitor numbers, your Google Business Profile and your Zillow profile as they appear today (the 5.0 from 11 reviews and the 68 sales come from a web search result, and I didn't open Zillow), your Instagram and Facebook pages, whether your forms reach you every time (my message through the company's form did reach you), and how the pages look on a real phone: I tested the page width in a desktop browser only. I loaded your agent page, three other pages on huntingtonandellis.com, and three pages on lasvegasluxuryrealtor.com.

What I checked on your site

  • Found
    Pages can be read and indexed

    Your agent page, home page and testimonials page all return 200 and have one canonical address each. robots.txt blocks only account, API and internal paths, and has a Sitemap line. Your agent page is in /sitemap-agent-dpages.xml.

  • Found
    llms.txt lists you

    huntingtonandellis.com/llms.txt is a plain-text summary of the brokerage with a list of agent pages, and yours is one of them.

  • Gap
    llms.txt reads your name as a city

    Its line for your page reads “Agent listing page featuring two residential properties in Phoenix.” It describes the page by your first name, as if it were the city, and doesn't mention Las Vegas.

  • Gap
    Your agent page's title and description don't say what you do or where

    The title is “Phoenix Sun” and the description is “REALTOR® | The Craig Tann Group”. Your own site's home page title reads “Phoenix Sun | Your Las Vegas, NV Real Estate Expert”.

  • Gap
    No business markup on either site

    I found no schema.org markup (the structured data that names a business, its address and its reviews) on the pages I loaded on either site. Lighthouse didn't test for it, so this is my read of the page code.

  • Gap
    Your bio is word for word on both sites

    “Originally from San Diego, I bring a personalized and innovative approach to real estate...” appears on your agent page, your own home page and your About page. I can't tell how Google treats the same text on two web addresses; I'm only reporting that it's the same.

  • Gap
    lasvegasluxuryrealtor.com has no llms.txt

    The address returns a “404 Page Not Found” page.

  • Gap
    A draft page is listed in the company's sitemap

    huntingtonandellis.com/sitemap-static.xml lists /draft-hendersons-landing-page-10022026, and it loads (200), titled “Copy of Henderson's Newest Home for Huntington & Ellis”. That one is also for your broker's web team.

These are plain checks anyone can repeat on October 18, 2026: what the site publishes, not a score.
